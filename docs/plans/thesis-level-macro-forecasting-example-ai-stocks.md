# 1. The core thesis to forecast

This portfolio looks less like “buy megacap AI” and more like:

> **AI scaling creates a sustained shortage of compute, power, data-center capacity, memory/storage, and energy infrastructure. The best equity returns accrue to constrained bottleneck owners, not necessarily only to model labs or NVDA.**

So the desk should forecast whether the world continues moving through this causal chain:

```text
AI capability progress
        ↓
Frontier model training + inference demand rises
        ↓
Hyperscaler / neocloud capex accelerates
        ↓
GPU, power, land, interconnect, memory, storage, and data-center capacity become bottlenecks
        ↓
Specialized infra companies get long-term contracts, pricing power, and capital access
        ↓
Equities like NBIS, CRWV, BE, IREN, CORZ, APLD, SNDK, etc. outperform
```

The research desk should therefore maintain **forecast ladders** across:

1. **AI demand / capability**
2. **Compute capex**
3. **Power and data-center bottlenecks**
4. **Company-specific execution**
5. **Valuation durability**
6. **Portfolio behavior / 13F meta-signal**
7. **Rotation / thesis-failure triggers**

# 10. The desk’s strategic output

The point is not just to answer questions. The desk should produce a rolling view like:

```text
AI infra thesis health: 74% intact through 2026
Power bottleneck thesis: 68% likely
Neocloud scarcity-rent thesis: 61% likely
Miner-to-HPC thesis: 44% likely
Memory/storage AI cycle thesis: 57% likely
Overbuild by mid-2027: 36% likely
```

Then translate that into stock-relevance:

```text
If power bottleneck probability rises → BE/APLD/IREN/CORZ become better suited.
If neocloud scarcity rises → NBIS/CRWV become better suited.
If storage/memory cycle rises → SNDK/MU become better suited.
If overbuild probability rises → reduce high-multiple infra beta.
If capex guide-down probability rises → reduce entire AI-infra complex.
```

That’s the shift from “forecasting basic questions” to a real **forecast-driven strategy desk**: every position is tied to explicit causal forecasts, update triggers, and falsification tests.


Yes — exactly. The binary questions tell us **whether** the thesis clears a threshold. The distributional siblings tell us **how much**, **how uncertain**, **where the tails are**, and **what risk we are actually underwriting**.

For a strategy/research desk, I’d structure most important thesis questions as pairs:

```text
Binary question:
Will X exceed threshold T by date D?

Distributional sibling:
What will the full distribution of X be by date D?
```

The binary question supports decision triggers.  
The distributional question supports sizing, risk, expected value, and scenario analysis.

# 25. The strategic benefit

Binary questions help answer:

```text
Should we act?
```

Distributional questions help answer:

```text
How big should the position be?
How much left-tail risk are we taking?
Is the upside convex?
Is this name a core holding or an option-like satellite?
How long can the trade last?
Which bottleneck is underpriced?
```

For an AI investment thesis, the distributional layer is essential because the trade is highly nonlinear:

- If capex grows modestly, semis may win but speculative infra may not.
- If capex explodes, NBIS/CRWV/BE/APLD/IREN/CORZ may have huge upside.
- If capex is strong but monetization disappoints, the trade may work short term and fail later.
- If power becomes the bottleneck, BE/power names may outperform GPU-cloud names.
- If GPU supply catches up, neocloud margins may compress even while AI adoption continues.
- If memory/storage pricing spikes, SNDK/MU may be better expressions than NVDA/CRWV.

So yes: keep the binary questions, but pair each with a distributional sibling. That is how the system becomes a true **forecast-driven strategy and risk desk**, not just a prediction tracker.
